Need workload where LFU wins: stable hot experts with occasional bursts/long gaps? Let's reason.

LFU with decay per epoch: maintain hit counts per expert, decay counts each epoch (e.g., multiply by alpha or reset windows). Evict lowest decayed frequency. It favors experts with high cumulative recent popularity, not just last access. LRU favors recency.

LFU wins when expert popularity is stable and access patterns have long reuse distances but high frequency: e.g., MoE routing dominated by a small set of experts used repeatedly across many tokens, but interleaved with many cold experts or periodic bursts. LRU may evict hot expert after a burst of cold experts because it becomes least recently used despite high overall frequency; LFU keeps it due to high decayed count. Also if cache slots limited and uploads expensive, keeping frequently used experts reduces uploads. Example: token sequence alternates hot experts A,B,C with many one-off experts D,E,F...; hot experts have high frequency but last access may be older than cold experts. LRU evicts hot after cold burst; LFU retains hot.

LFU loses when locality/recency matters more than frequency: e.g., phase-shifting workload, experts become hot then cold, or sequential scan through experts with no reuse. Pure LRU adapts quickly to current phase; LFU with decay may keep stale experts because their historical counts remain high, causing thrashing and evicting newly hot experts. Example: workload has phases: first phase uses experts 1..K heavily, then phase 2 uses experts K+1..2K heavily. If decay too slow, LFU keeps phase 1 experts, evicts phase 2; LRU quickly discards phase 1. Also if access is recency-biased with low frequency but high temporal locality (e.g., repeated use of same expert in a short burst then never again), LRU better.
